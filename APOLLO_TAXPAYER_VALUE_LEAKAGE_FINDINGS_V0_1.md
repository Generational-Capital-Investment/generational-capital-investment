# Apollo Taxpayer Value Leakage Findings — V0.1

**Generational Capital Investment (GCI)**  
**Status:** Interim research finding / evidence-building phase  
**Companion document:** `APOLLO_AS_GENERATIONAL_INVESTMENT_LITMUS_TEST.md`

---

## Executive Summary

The Apollo research began with a narrow question:

> **If taxpayers financed a generational public mission that created, materially advanced, or de-risked technologies later commercialized by private industry, could the public have retained a modest direct financial participation in the commercial value that followed?**

The first phase of research does **not** support the proposition that a realistic direct-return structure would have repaid the full historical cost of Apollo.

It does support a different and increasingly important finding:

> **Apollo repeatedly created, matured, validated, scaled, or de-risked commercially valuable technology and know-how, yet the public financial interest generally did not continue as that value moved into private commercialization.**

The recurring issue is not lack of value creation. The issue is **value leakage**.

Across the cases researched so far, taxpayer-supported value moved through private ownership, licensing, sublicensing, commercial derivatives, employee and know-how migration, and later intellectual-property transactions. Private firms continued to recognize, price, license, sell, and retain economic interests in that value. The originating public capital generally did not retain an equivalent continuing financial claim.

This is a counterfactual institutional finding, not an allegation of historical wrongdoing. The companies involved operated under the laws and agreements in force at the time. GCI asks whether future public-investment structures should be designed differently.

The emerging Apollo lesson is therefore:

> **Public capital can create lasting private commercial value at multiple points in a technology's life, but without an explicit value-retention architecture the taxpayer's direct economic interest can fall to zero long before the value itself disappears.**

---

## 1. Research Posture

This document is an **interim finding**, not a final return estimate.

The evidence rules established in the Apollo litmus-test document remain controlling:

- historical fact and counterfactual modeling stay separate;
- public-program connection does not automatically establish public ownership;
- private maturation and private risk must be recognized;
- company-wide revenue cannot be attributed to an Apollo-derived technology without evidence;
- derivative lineage must be established before revenue is modeled;
- observed, derived, estimated, assumed, and counterfactual values remain separately labeled;
- failure to recover Apollo's full cost is an acceptable research result.

The purpose of this document is to identify a pattern that has emerged across multiple researched cases: **where public economic value appears to leave the public financial chain.**

---

## 2. What “Taxpayer Value Leakage” Means

For this research, **taxpayer value leakage** means:

> A point at which technology, know-how, productive capability, intellectual property, or another economically valuable asset materially created, matured, validated, scaled, or de-risked through public capital continues to generate private commercial value while no corresponding continuing public financial participation has been identified.

Leakage does **not** mean theft, contractual breach, unlawful privatization, that government legally owned every resulting invention, that private companies did not contribute substantial capital and innovation, or that taxpayers were historically entitled to royalties that were never negotiated.

The term describes an **institutional design gap**.

Private commercial value can persist for decades after the public contribution. If public participation ends automatically when commercialization begins, the economic value of the public contribution is effectively assigned a financial value of zero at precisely the point the technology begins producing private revenue.

---

## 3. The Apollo Value Chain

The working commercialization chain is:

**Taxpayer Capital**  
↓  
**Public Mission / Procurement / Development Requirement**  
↓  
**Technology, Know-How, Manufacturing Capability, Data, or IP**  
↓  
**Private Control or Commercial Rights**  
↓  
**Licensing / Sublicensing / Technology Transfer**  
↓  
**Commercial Product**  
↓  
**Derivative Product or Successor Technology**  
↓  
**IP Sale / Corporate Transfer / Acquisition**  
↓  
**Long-Tail Commercial Revenue**

The current research has found potential public-value leakage at nearly every stage after the initial public contribution.

---

## 4. Leakage Type 1 — IP / Control Transfer

### Case: IBM / Information Management System (IMS)

IBM's own history traces IMS to the Apollo program's requirement for an automated system capable of managing the enormous bills of material and engineering changes associated with the spacecraft program. IBM, Rockwell, and Caterpillar personnel jointly developed the original system. IBM later commercialized it as IMS.

**Research pathway:**

**Apollo requirement**  
→ **joint Rockwell / IBM development**  
→ **commercially valuable database capability**  
→ **rights negotiation**  
→ **Rockwell relinquishes continuing product rights**  
→ **IBM commercializes IMS for decades**

The important finding is not that IBM improperly obtained the product. It is that a capability developed to solve an Apollo requirement became a durable private commercial product while no continuing taxpayer participation right has been identified.

The private rights did not vanish. They were negotiated.

The public economic interest did.

### Why this matters

This is the simplest form of leakage:

> **A public mission materially contributes to creation of a commercial asset, but the public participation right does not survive the transition into private commercial control.**

### Current quantitative status

The provisional GCI model currently treats IBM/IMS as the largest quantified Apollo branch. Under the project's current **4% market-conservative counterfactual**, the accepted IMS revenue reconstruction produces approximately **$480 million in modeled direct public receipts**.

This is a counterfactual model output, not historical revenue received by the government.

---

## 5. Leakage Type 2 — Private Sublicensing / Royalty Capture

### Case: Fairchild Semiconductor → Philco-Ford

Apollo was a major early customer for integrated circuits. The research does **not** claim that Apollo invented the integrated circuit or Fairchild's foundational planar process.

The public contribution was different:

- exceptional early procurement volume;
- production scaling;
- reliability qualification;
- testing and failure analysis;
- manufacturing learning;
- falling unit costs;
- technical assistance and process improvement.

Fairchild later cross-licensed semiconductor technology and manufacturing know-how to Philco-Ford for production associated with Apollo Block II systems. Historical evidence shows that Fairchild received royalties from the arrangement.

**Research pathway:**

**Apollo demand and maturation**  
→ **Fairchild technology / know-how**  
→ **private cross-license to Philco-Ford**  
→ **Philco manufacturing**  
→ **Fairchild royalty income**

No equivalent continuing taxpayer royalty has been identified.

### Why this matters

The private market itself recognized that the originating technology contribution retained economic value after transfer.

That value was not theoretical. It was priced through a royalty.

This creates one of the sharpest questions in the GCI research:

> **If private parties recognized that the technology originator deserved continuing compensation, why was the public contribution automatically assigned no comparable continuing financial interest?**

The exact Philco royalty rate remains unresolved. Fairchild's later approximately 4% domestic planar-process royalty benchmark is retained only as market context and is not treated as the Philco rate.

## 6. Leakage Type 3 — Derivative Commercialization

A public contribution can remain economically meaningful even when the commercial product is not identical to the original mission hardware.

### Tylan — Mass-Flow Control

NASA historical records trace Tylan's commercial mass-flow-control technology to low-gas-flow technology developed to meet Apollo-related requirements.

**small Apollo requirement**  
→ **commercialized mass-flow technology**  
→ **industrial applications**  
→ **semiconductor-manufacturing market**

The accepted research currently models only one approximately **$9 million annual commercial-product-value snapshot**, despite evidence of a much longer commercial life.

### Delco / AC Electronics — Carousel IV

NASA explicitly documented that inertial-navigation hardware designs and expertise developed for Apollo and the Lunar Module were used by General Motors' Delco organization to develop the Carousel IV commercial aircraft inertial-navigation product line.

The current research uses only a conservative **$38 million lower-bound commercial equipment-value floor**, despite documented use by dozens of airlines and hundreds of commercial aircraft.

### Fairchild / Burroughs CTL

The research connects Apollo-matured silicon planar-epitaxial manufacturing capability to Fairchild's later CTL products for Burroughs commercial computers.

Because CTL represented substantial additional private development, the branch is treated as a more distant derivative rather than a direct Apollo product.

### Why these cases matter

> **The public claim can disappear at the first commercial boundary even though the public contribution remains materially embedded in the productive capability that makes later products possible.**

GCI therefore requires a declining **derivative-distance** framework rather than a binary “Apollo” / “not Apollo” rule.

---

## 7. Leakage Type 4 — Know-How and Human-Capital Migration

### Case: Fairchild → General Microelectronics and the semiconductor ecosystem

Not all valuable technology is embodied in a patent.

Publicly financed programs can create process knowledge, production techniques, reliability practices, failure-analysis methods, design judgment, manufacturing expertise, and trained engineering teams.

The semiconductor research shows people and technical knowledge moving from Fairchild into newly formed companies, including General Microelectronics (GMe).

Some adjacent GMe and AMI research is NASA-related but not yet financially attributable to Apollo, so those branches remain **attribution unresolved** rather than included in the Apollo financial portfolio.

### Why this matters

> **Know-how can leave the public value chain without a patent ever changing hands.**

Any modern value-retention architecture must therefore distinguish legal IP from productive know-how, data, manufacturing methods, and government-funded validation.

---

## 8. Leakage Type 5 — Later IP Monetization

### Case: UTC → Ballard Power Systems

The fuel-cell lineage provides a later-generation example of private value retention.

In 2014 Ballard acquired a UTC fuel-cell portfolio containing approximately 800 patents and patent applications plus licenses, invention disclosures, and know-how for total consideration of approximately **$22.3 million**.

UTC did not simply sell the assets and surrender all future value. It retained cash and equity consideration, a grant-back license, and a continuing share—typically about **25%**—of future IP sale and licensing income for 15 years.

When Ballard later transferred automotive-related IP to Volkswagen, that continuing participation produced additional payments to UTC.

This later portfolio consisted substantially of post-Apollo technology and is **not** treated wholesale as Apollo-derived revenue.

Its importance is structural.

### Why this matters

> **Transfer the technology, preserve commercial freedom, and still retain a defined long-tail economic participation.**

Continuing participation is normal private economic behavior when valuable technology rights are transferred.

---

## 9. Leakage Type 6 — Publicly Financed Refinement

### Case: Black & Decker

Black & Decker had cordless-tool technology before Apollo. NASA did not invent the cordless drill.

During Apollo lunar-drill work, however, Black & Decker used a specially developed computer program to optimize the drill's motor for minimum power consumption and gained additional experience in battery-powered equipment. NASA later traced refinement of this technology into the Dustbuster and other cordless products.

**pre-existing private cordless technology**  
→ **Apollo lunar-drill requirement**  
→ **publicly financed motor / efficiency / engineering refinement**  
→ **stronger private cordless-technology base**  
→ **later commercial products**

The commercial scale was substantial, but the current research does not yet have a manufacturer-level revenue base suitable for inclusion in the GCI financial model.

### Why this matters

Public value creation does not require public invention.

A value-retention framework that recognizes only government-owned patents would miss publicly financed improvements in efficiency, reliability, manufacturability, testing, miniaturization, durability, and commercial readiness.

---

## 10. Special Case — Public Technology Diffusion Without Financial Participation

### Pratt & Whitney / United Aircraft Fuel Cells

Apollo fuel cells provide the strongest rights-and-control evidence discovered so far.

NASA historical records document a **$90 million Apollo fuel-cell development and manufacturing contract** to Pratt & Whitney after the company's earlier privately financed commercial effort was nearing abandonment.

A later federal antitrust consent judgment formally defined a body of **“Apollo Technology”** arising from Apollo fuel-cell contracts. The defined technology included engineering and manufacturing information such as blueprints, specifications, laboratory results, test and operating data, and manufacturing-cost information.

The judgment required access to covered technology and royalty-free licensing of certain patents.

The policy remedy was therefore **technology diffusion and competition**, rather than **continuing taxpayer financial participation**.

### Why this matters

This case shows that Apollo-derived economic value was identifiable enough for the federal government and a federal court to define a transferable technology bundle.

GCI asks whether broad technology diffusion and modest public financial participation could coexist.

---

## 11. The Pattern Across Cases

The cases are technologically different: enterprise software, semiconductors, fuel cells, industrial flow control, aircraft navigation, and consumer cordless products.

Yet the financial pattern repeats.

### Public value enters the system

Taxpayer capital provides some combination of development funding, procurement, mission demand, testing, reliability qualification, manufacturing scale, technical assistance, process improvement, market validation, and risk absorption.

### Private value remains visible

Private firms later recognize economic value through product sales, royalties, cross-licenses, patents, know-how transfers, IP sales, retained licenses, and retained future licensing participation.

### Public financial participation generally disappears

The taxpayer's direct interest is generally not carried forward through those same transactions.

That is the central Apollo finding.

## 12. What the Current Numeric Model Says — and Does Not Say

The current accepted numeric portfolio contains four conservatively quantified branches:

- IBM / IMS;
- Fairchild / Burroughs CTL;
- Tylan mass-flow control;
- Delco / Carousel IV.

Under the current **4% market-conservative participation assumption**, with the project's time-decay and derivative-distance rules applied, Base44 currently reports approximately:

- **$483 million in modeled direct public receipts**;
- **$1.25 billion in hypothetical 2026 GCI fund value** if receipts remained invested at a 4% annual return;
- approximately **1.87% nominal direct recovery** against the project's full $25.8 billion historical Apollo denominator.

These figures are provisional model outputs and deliberately incomplete.

They currently exclude or understate Pratt & Whitney / fuel cells, most of Fairchild's commercial tail, Black & Decker, unresearched Apollo derivatives, and revenue years for already qualified cases where acceptable evidence has not yet been reconstructed.

They also exclude broad indirect benefits such as GDP, jobs, tax receipts, productivity gains, national capability, scientific knowledge, strategic value, and quality-of-life gains.

The direct-return model is an **additional ledger**, not a substitute for those benefits.

---

## 13. The Apollo Finding Is Not “Apollo Could Have Paid for Itself”

The evidence does not currently support that conclusion.

Nor does the GCI hypothesis require it.

A more defensible finding is:

> **A properly structured public participation system could have returned meaningful, measurable, reusable capital to taxpayers while preserving the overwhelming majority of private commercial value for the companies that matured, manufactured, marketed, and continued to innovate around the technology.**

The question is not whether Apollo's direct commercial return could replace its social, scientific, strategic, or economic returns.

The question is why the direct return had to be **zero or nearly zero** when private actors downstream clearly recognized that technology rights retained financial value.

---

## 14. The Reinvestment Insight

Under the working GCI model:

**commercial participation receipts**  
→ **return to a protected public investment fund**  
→ **remain invested**  
→ **compound**  
→ **finance later generational projects**

This does not transform Apollo into a solution for the national debt or Social Security. The scale demonstrated so far is far too small for such claims.

The more disciplined proposition is:

> **Public-investment returns should remain productive public capital so the next generation of long-horizon investment does not have to begin entirely from zero.**

---

## 15. Working Research Conclusion

The current evidence supports the following interim conclusion:

> **Apollo demonstrates repeated points at which taxpayer-funded or taxpayer-matured economic value continued into private commercialization while the public financial interest generally did not continue with it. Private actors subsequently priced, licensed, sublicensed, sold, and retained economic interests in that value. The GCI hypothesis asks whether a small, finite, predictable, and declining public participation right could have preserved some of that value for reinvestment without materially reducing private-sector incentives.**

This conclusion remains open to challenge.

Future research may identify public participation that has not yet been found, weaken attribution in individual cases, strengthen other cases, materially change the modeled return, or identify costs and unintended consequences not yet captured.

The standard remains:

> **Take it. Break it. Improve it.**

## 16. Next Research Questions

1. How often did Apollo contracts explicitly allocate commercial patent and data rights?
2. Were there cases where NASA **did** retain meaningful financial rights, and what happened?
3. What mechanisms prevented or discouraged public royalty retention?
4. How would a public participation right have affected contractor bids and product pricing?
5. Could royalty obligations have been priced transparently into commercial products without materially slowing adoption?
6. What legal treatment would be required when public funding only **refines** private technology?
7. How should rights follow sublicensing, IP sales, acquisitions, bankruptcy, or corporate restructuring?
8. At what derivative distance should public participation terminate?
9. How should open diffusion and competition be preserved while retaining public financial value?
10. What would the same leakage analysis show when applied to a modern program such as clean-energy demonstration and infrastructure technology funding?
